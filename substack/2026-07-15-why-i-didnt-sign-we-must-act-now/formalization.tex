\begin{abstract}
We formalize Ben Goertzel's ``Why I Didn't Sign `We Must Act Now'''
(2026-07-15) within the Hyperseed ontology, incorporating d-calculus
extensions from Hyperseed v2. Surface/directed disagreement is modeled
as 0-cell/1-cell curvature (agenda smuggling severity), centralized
steering as frozen directed type with diversity-loss holonomy,
prediction impossibility as high prediction-horizon curvature,
decentralized development as parallel transport across many fibers,
and complementarity as an emergent sheaf with specification-gap
curvature.
\end{abstract}

\section{Surface agreement masking directed disagreement}

\begin{theorem}[0-cell vs 1-cell content --- claims 1--2, 6, 10]
\label{thm:surface}
The letter's content stratifies by directed-type level:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{0-cells (surface).} ``AI is powerful,'' ``economy will
    change,'' ``we should act.'' Point-level observations with near-universal
    agreement.
  \item \textbf{1-cells (directed).} ``Steer AI to complement humans''
    $=$ ``steer away from autonomous AGI toward tools-only'' (Acemoglu
    school). Directed paths from the shared observations to specific
    policy conclusions.
\end{itemize}
The letter exploits 0-cell consensus to smuggle 1-cell agenda: by
framing the 1-cell content as if it follows inevitably from the 0-cell
consensus, a specific directed path is presented as the only path from
the shared starting points.

\textbf{Genre.} The same technique appears in the tobacco ``Frank
Statement'' (1954), Oregon Petition (1998), and Pause Giant AI (2023):
blandly agreeable 0-cell content carrying sharper 1-cell agenda.
\end{theorem}

\section{Centralized steering as frozen directed type}

\begin{proposition}[Committee-frozen trajectory --- claims 3--5, 11--12]
\label{prop:frozen}
An expert committee that steers AI development freezes the directed type:
\[
  \mathcal{D}_{\text{steered}} \subset \mathcal{D}_{\text{organic}}
\]
The committee selects which development paths to allow, constraining the
set of possible trajectories. This is the same frozen-directed-type
pathology as the ``eternal regime'' flavor (Seven Flavors): a small
group captures the development trajectory.

\textbf{Prediction impossibility.} The committee's path selection is
based on undecidable information --- nobody can predict which jobs AGI
will replace, at what point, with what impact. The steam engine analogy:
\[
  \text{committee}(1760, \text{steam engine}) \to \text{policy}
  \quad \text{is structurally guaranteed wrong}
\]
because the information needed to select correctly doesn't exist at
the time of selection.
\end{proposition}

\section{Decentralized development as parallel transport}

\begin{proposition}[Anti-scalar-collapse governance --- claims 7--9, 13]
\label{prop:parallel}
Decentralized development is parallel transport across many independent
fibers:
\[
  \{P_{\gamma_i}: F_{b_0} \to F_{b_i}\}_{i=1}^n \quad \text{(many
    independent transport paths)}
\]
Different architectures, value systems, cultures, and economic models
each transport the AI development fiber along different paths.
Collapsing to a single committee-approved path is scalar collapse:
\[
  \{P_{\gamma_1}, \ldots, P_{\gamma_n}\} \xrightarrow{\text{committee}}
    P_{\gamma_{\text{approved}}} \quad \text{(scalar collapse)}
\]
reducing a rich multi-dimensional development space to a single
dimension controlled by a single authority.
\end{proposition}

\section{Complementarity as emergent sheaf}

\begin{proposition}[Emergent global section --- claims 8--9, 15]
\label{prop:sheaf}
Human-AI complementarity is a global section of a sheaf:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Local interactions.} Billions of human-AI interactions in
    specific contexts produce local complementarity data: in context $U_i$,
    human does $X_i$ and AI does $Y_i$, and the combination works.
  \item \textbf{Global section.} The overall complementarity pattern is
    the global section $s \in \Gamma(U, \mathcal{F})$ reconstructed from
    local sections $\{s_i\}$ via the sheaf condition.
  \item \textbf{Cannot be specified top-down.} The sheaf condition
    (global section consistent with all local data) cannot be satisfied
    by specification because the local data $\{s_i\}$ doesn't exist yet.
    You must generate it through actual interactions.
\end{enumerate}
Complementarity is a relationship worked out in practice, not a property
written into a policy brief.
\end{proposition}

\section{Deceptive letters as provenance manipulation}

\begin{proposition}[Broken provenance cues --- claim 6, 14]
\label{prop:provenance}
Prestigious signatures provide provenance cues that imply ``expert-verified
knowledge.'' But the signatures may endorse only the 0-cell content
while being cited as endorsing the 1-cell content:
\[
  \text{sign}(s_i, \text{0-cell}) \neq \text{endorse}(s_i, \text{1-cell})
\]
The provenance cue exists but doesn't carry the information it claims to
--- the same broken-provenance pattern as KYC (Fable Farce) and the same
provenance theatre identified across the Substack corpus.
\end{proposition}

\section{Prediction impossibility and the steam engine}
\label{sec:prediction}
%% =================================================================

\begin{proposition}[Unpredictable directed type --- claims 5--8]
\label{prop:predict}
Nobody can predict which jobs AGI will replace vs.\ complement, at what
point, with what capability, or what impact a given AI technology will
have after people pivot and adapt. The steam engine analogy: nobody in
the 1760s could have predicted its employment effects.

Between obvious extremes (plumbing robot replaces human; preschool
teacher stays human), there is an enormous space of intermediate cases
whose resolution will elude foresight.

In Hyperseed terms, the directed type of economic transformation is
\emph{inherently unpredictable} at the 1-cell level: while the 0-cells
(that transformation will occur) are visible, the specific directed
trajectories through which it unfolds cannot be computed in advance.
This is a fundamental property of sufficiently complex directed types
--- the space of possible 1-cells is too large and context-dependent
for any finite prediction model.

The implication for governance: any policy framework that presupposes
the ability to predict specific outcomes (``steer AI toward X'') is
operating on a fiction. The honest approach is to create conditions for
robust adaptation rather than to prescribe trajectories --- exactly
the design philosophy of robust sheaf structures that work with
arbitrary local sections rather than requiring specific ones.
\end{proposition}

\begin{conjecture}[Steering impossibility theorem]
Conjecture: for any sufficiently complex technology (complexity measured
by the dimension of its directed type), there exists no finite committee
whose predictions about the technology's socioeconomic impact are
reliably better than the collective predictions emerging from
decentralized experimentation. Formally: the prediction error of any
centralized model grows superlinearly with the dimension of the
technology's directed type, while the collective prediction error from
decentralized exploration grows sublinearly. The crossover point ---
where decentralized exploration becomes more reliable than centralized
prediction --- occurs well before the technology reaches AGI-level
complexity.
\end{conjecture}

\section{d-calculus formulation (Hyperseed v2)}

\begin{proposition}[Surface-depth curvature --- claim 16]
\label{prop:surface_curv}
\[
  \|F_\nabla(\text{0-cell}, \text{1-cell})\| = \text{agenda smuggling
    severity}
\]
High curvature: the 1-cell content (specific policy agenda) diverges
sharply from what the 0-cell content (bland truisms) implies. Low
curvature: the 1-cell content follows naturally from the 0-cell content.
The article argues this curvature is high --- the letter's specific
agenda doesn't follow from its bland premises.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Development trajectory holonomy --- claim 17]
\label{prop:traj_hol}
A loop through the steering cycle $\gamma$: committee convenes $\to$
restricts paths $\to$ observes outcomes $\to$ reconvenes produces holonomy:
\[
  \operatorname{Hol}_\gamma(\Gamma_{\text{steering}}) = \text{development
    diversity loss per steering cycle}
\]
\begin{itemize}
  \item Increasing holonomy: each cycle reduces diversity more (path
    concentration, increasing homogeneity).
  \item The decentralized alternative targets zero holonomy: each cycle
    maintains or increases diversity.
\end{itemize}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Prediction uncertainty curvature --- claim 18]
\label{prop:pred_curv}
\[
  \|F_\nabla(\text{prediction}, \text{horizon})\| = \text{prediction
    degradation rate}
\]
High curvature: prediction confidence drops rapidly with time horizon
(the actual situation for AGI impact --- nobody can predict even a few
years out). This high curvature is the formal content of the steam
engine analogy: the curvature is too high for committee steering to
work.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Complementarity emergence curvature --- claim 19]
\label{prop:comp_curv}
\[
  \|F_\nabla(\text{specified}, \text{emergent})\| = \text{specification gap}
\]
High curvature: specified complementarity diverges from emergent
complementarity (the specification misses what actually works). Low
curvature: specification tracks emergence. The article argues this
curvature is high --- you can't specify complementarity in advance,
you must let it emerge from practice.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Provenance manipulation gradient --- claim 20]
\label{prop:prov_grad}
\[
  \nabla_{\text{provenance}} = \nabla\left(\text{signature\_authenticity}
    \times \text{claim\_endorsement} \times
    \text{agenda\_transparency}\right)
\]
Steep gradient: some dimensions reliable (signatures are real) while
others unreliable (claims aren't genuinely endorsed, agenda isn't
transparent). The deceptive-letter genre exploits the steep gradient:
using reliable dimensions to imply reliability in unreliable dimensions.
\end{proposition}
