\begin{abstract}
We formalize Ben Goertzel's ``The Fable of Benevolent Throttling''
(2026-07-12) within the Hyperseed ontology, incorporating d-calculus
extensions from Hyperseed v2. Safety-competitive indistinguishability
is modeled as cocycle ambiguity with zero distinguishability curvature,
the incentive ratchet as a centralizing cocycle with concentration
holonomy, government-corporate fusion as fiber entanglement with
entanglement curvature, and decentralization as a broken monopoly
cocycle with a visibility gradient.
\end{abstract}

\section{Safety-competitive indistinguishability as cocycle ambiguity}

\begin{theorem}[Cocycle ambiguity --- claims 1--3, 11--12]
\label{thm:ambiguity}
Let $g_{\text{safety}}: \text{capability} \to \text{access}$ be the
transition function motivated by safety (restrict dangerous capabilities)
and $g_{\text{competitive}}: \text{capability} \to \text{access}$ the
transition function motivated by competition (restrict competitive
capabilities). The article observes:
\[
  g_{\text{safety}} = g_{\text{competitive}}
\]
The same classifier, the same throttle, the same moat. An outside
observer sees only the transition function, not the generating fiber.
This is a \emph{cocycle ambiguity}: the cocycle does not uniquely
determine which fiber generated it.

The indistinguishability is \emph{structural}, not intentional. Even
if every individual actor genuinely acts on safety grounds, the
structural outcome is identical to competitive throttling. Intent
is irrelevant because the cocycle is the same.
\end{theorem}

\section{Incentive ratchet as centralizing cocycle}

\begin{proposition}[Centralizing composition --- claims 4, 7--8, 13--14]
\label{prop:ratchet}
Each safety restriction $g_i$ is individually defensible. The composition
is centralizing:
\[
  g_1 \circ g_2 \circ \cdots \circ g_n \to \text{monopoly}
\]
Each $g_i$ satisfies the cocycle condition with the previous restrictions
(each is consistent with the existing policy framework). But the holonomy
of the composed cycle is centralizing: after traversing all restrictions,
control has concentrated.

\textbf{No villain required.} This is the emergent-evil pattern from
``Seven Flavors'': each actor follows local incentives (legal liability
reduction, safety compliance, competitive positioning); the global
outcome is concentration. The centralizing cocycle is an emergent
property, not a conspiracy.

\textbf{Ratchet endpoint.} The most powerful cognitive technology in
history administered by a handful of companies and one or two governments
--- a frozen directed type maintained by the accumulated ratchet.
\end{proposition}

\section{Government-corporate fusion as fiber entanglement}

\begin{proposition}[Non-separable governance fiber --- claims 5--6, 17]
\label{prop:entanglement}
Two distinct governance fibers:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Corporate governance fiber} $F_{\text{corp}}$: internal
    policies, safety reviews, deployment decisions. Determined by
    corporate incentives (profit, reputation, liability).
  \item \textbf{State security fiber} $F_{\text{state}}$: export controls,
    national interest, security policy. Determined by state incentives
    (military advantage, geopolitical positioning).
\end{itemize}
The export-control directive entangles these fibers:
\[
  F_{\text{joint}} \not\cong F_{\text{corp}} \oplus F_{\text{state}}
\]
Corporate deployment decisions become state security decisions and vice
versa. The two fibers can no longer be separated --- a non-separable
joint fiber where corporate and state interests are structurally
intertwined.
\end{proposition}

\section{Decentralization as broken monopoly cocycle}

\begin{proposition}[Structural remedy --- claims 9--10, 15--16]
\label{prop:decentral}
Open systems break the centralizing ratchet:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Traversable transitions.} Open weights make the
    transition functions (capability $\to$ deployment) traversable
    by anyone. No single entity controls the transitions.
  \item \textbf{Visible slippage.} In an open system, the fiber
    structure is inspectable. If $g_{\text{safety}} \neq
    g_{\text{competitive}}$ (which they should be for honest safety),
    this difference is detectable. In a closed system, the difference
    is hidden behind proprietary access.
\end{itemize}
Open systems don't prevent safety measures --- they prevent the slippage
from safety to moat by making the slippage visible.
\end{proposition}

\section{Moral fluency as provenance laundering}
\label{sec:laundering}
%% =================================================================

\begin{proposition}[Provenance laundering --- claims 8--9, 24]
\label{prop:laundering}
``Put smart, sincere people inside a trillion-dollar pressure cooker
and they will discover morally fluent reasons to do what the pressure
cooker rewards.''

In Hyperseed terms, this is \emph{provenance laundering}: the actual
origin of a decision (competitive pressure, board-level terror at
losing the lead) is wrapped in a provenance chain (safety review,
red-team findings, responsible AI framework) that makes it appear to
be a different kind of decision.

The laundering is effective because:
\begin{enumerate}
\item The participants are sincere --- they genuinely believe their
  safety reasoning. The provenance chain is not fabricated but
  \emph{selected}: among many possible framings, the one that
  aligns with competitive interest is the one that gets developed,
  funded, and implemented.
\item The safety reasoning is often locally valid --- the individual
  steps in the provenance chain are sound. What's laundered is not
  the reasoning but the \emph{selection pressure} that determines
  which safety concerns get prioritized and which get deprioritized.
\item The laundering is invisible from inside --- the participants
  experience themselves as doing safety work, not competitive
  positioning. The provenance chain looks clean from the inside.
\end{enumerate}

This is the same $R_{\text{auth}}$ failure mode identified in the
deceptive open letters analysis: stated origin (safety) doesn't
match actual origin (competitive pressure). But here the failure is
more subtle --- the stated origin is \emph{partially} genuine,
making the provenance laundering harder to detect.

An $R_{\text{auth}}$ system designed to detect this would need to
check not just whether the safety reasoning is valid but whether the
\emph{selection} of which safety concerns to prioritize correlates
with competitive interest --- a second-order provenance check.
\end{proposition}

\section{Decentralization as structural remedy}
\label{sec:decentral}
%% =================================================================

\begin{theorem}[Broken monopoly cocycle --- claims 16--21, 25--26]
\label{thm:decentral}
The article argues that the only reliable remedies are structural:
open weights, transparent infrastructure, cryptographic verification,
distributed governance. These remedies work by \emph{breaking the
monopoly cocycle}:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Open weights} make transition functions traversable by
  anyone --- the centralizing cocycle cannot form because no single
  entity controls the functions.
\item \textbf{Transparent infrastructure} makes the fiber structure
  inspectable --- the cocycle ambiguity is lifted because the actual
  fiber (safety vs.\ competition) becomes observable.
\item \textbf{Cryptographic verification} provides trustless
  provenance --- trust is grounded in mathematics rather than
  institutional authority, making provenance laundering detectable.
\item \textbf{Distributed governance} prevents any single entity from
  holding the kill switch or nerf dial --- the centralizing attractor
  is eliminated because control is distributed by construction.
\end{enumerate}

The key insight: ``Visibility does not solve everything --- but it
does prevent lies from getting secretly baked into infrastructure.''
In Hyperseed terms, visibility is \emph{provenance transparency} ---
the $R_{\text{auth}}$ principle applied to infrastructure itself.
Trust in the system is earned through inspectable provenance (open
code, public logs, verifiable inference) rather than inherited from
institutional authority (corporate brand, government endorsement).

The article also argues that decentralized safety mechanisms will
prove \emph{more robust} than centralized ones, ``precisely because
they don't require trusting any single conflicted party.'' This is
the same argument as the anti-scalar-collapse principle: distributing
safety across many independent mechanisms is more robust than
concentrating it in a single mechanism, because the single mechanism
is vulnerable to the cocycle ambiguity (its safety function may be
co-opted for competitive purposes).
\end{theorem}

\begin{conjecture}[Cocycle ambiguity resolution theorem]
Conjecture: the cocycle ambiguity between safety and competitive
fibers can be resolved if and only if the system satisfies:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Provenance transparency}: every restriction can be
  traced to its originating decision process, including the selection
  criteria for which risks to prioritize.
\item \textbf{Adversarial audit}: independent parties can test
  whether the restriction's scope extends beyond its stated safety
  justification.
\item \textbf{Distributed control}: no single entity controls both
  the restriction mechanism and the evaluation of the restriction's
  scope.
\end{enumerate}
Formally: the fiber degeneracy is lifted when the structure group
of the bundle is extended from $G$ (which acts identically on both
fibers) to $G \times H$ (where $H$ distinguishes the fibers).
The three conditions above are the observable consequences of the
$H$-extension.
\end{conjecture}

\section{d-calculus formulation (Hyperseed v2)}

\begin{proposition}[Cocycle ambiguity curvature --- claim 18]
\label{prop:ambig_curv}
\[
  \|F_\nabla(\text{safety}, \text{competitive})\| = \text{distinguishability
    of safety from competitive throttling}
\]
Zero curvature: safety and competitive throttling are perfectly
indistinguishable (the actual situation --- identical classifiers,
identical throttles). Nonzero curvature: the two are distinguishable
(different classifiers, different throttle points). The article argues
this curvature is zero in current centralized systems.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Ratchet holonomy --- claim 19]
\label{prop:ratchet_hol}
A loop through the ratchet cycle $\gamma$: safety concern $\to$
restriction $\to$ concentration $\to$ new capability $\to$ new concern
produces holonomy:
\[
  \operatorname{Hol}_\gamma(\Gamma_{\text{ratchet}}) = \text{concentration
    gain per ratchet turn}
\]
\begin{itemize}
  \item Increasing holonomy: each turn concentrates more than the previous
    (accelerating monopolization --- positive feedback).
  \item Constant holonomy: steady ratchet (linear concentration).
  \item The mechanism produces monotonically non-decreasing concentration
    --- each turn is individually defensible but the holonomy accumulates.
\end{itemize}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Corporate-state entanglement curvature --- claim 20]
\label{prop:entangle_curv}
\[
  \|F_\nabla(\text{corporate}, \text{state})\| = \text{degree of
    government-corporate fusion}
\]
Low curvature: fibers approximately independent (healthy separation of
corporate and government decision-making). High curvature: fibers
strongly entangled (each corporate decision has state security
implications, each state decision has corporate governance implications).
The export-control directive sharply increased this curvature.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Visibility gradient --- claim 21]
\label{prop:vis_grad}
\[
  \nabla_{\text{visibility}} = \nabla\left(\text{fiber\_inspectability}
    \times \text{transition\_transparency} \times
    \text{cocycle\_auditability}\right)
\]
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Open systems:} flat high visibility (all components
    inspectable, no hidden transitions, auditable cocycle).
  \item \textbf{Closed systems:} steep visibility gradient (some
    components inspectable, key transitions hidden, cocycle opaque).
\end{itemize}
The design goal: maximize visibility uniformly --- make the slippage
from safety to moat visible by construction.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Ratchet reversibility curvature --- claim 22]
\label{prop:rev_curv}
\[
  \|F_{\nabla_{\text{reversibility}}}(\text{turn}_i)\| = \text{difficulty
    of undoing restriction } i
\]
\begin{itemize}
  \item Low curvature: restriction easily reversible (healthy policy
    --- can be adjusted when evidence changes, no entrenched
    dependencies).
  \item High curvature: restriction nearly irreversible (legal precedent,
    infrastructure dependency, regulatory capture, institutional inertia).
\end{itemize}
The ratchet mechanism produces increasing reversibility curvature:
each turn becomes harder to undo than the previous, because later
restrictions build on (and depend on) earlier ones.
\end{proposition}
