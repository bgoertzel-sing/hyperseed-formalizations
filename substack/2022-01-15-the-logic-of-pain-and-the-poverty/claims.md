# Claim inventory — The Logic of Pain and the Poverty of Punishment

Source: Ben Goertzel, "The Logic of Pain and the Poverty of Punishment,"
Eurykosmotron, 2022-01-15.

## Epistemic-status key

- **source-paraphrase**: directly stated or closely implied in the article.
- **inferred**: not stated verbatim but follows from the article's logic.
- **hypothesis**: proposed by the article as a conjecture.
- **hyperseed-interpretation**: formalized within the Hyperseed ontology.

---

### Core claims from the article

1. **Pain as information signal.** Pain evolved as a damage-detection
   signal; the suffering component is an evolutionary side effect, not
   intrinsically necessary. *(source-paraphrase)*

2. **Signal-suffering dissociability.** The informational content of pain
   can be separated from the suffering experience, as modern medicine
   demonstrates. *(source-paraphrase)*

3. **Punishment as crude mechanism.** Punishment is a social analog of
   pain — aversive signal conflating deterrence with suffering. Often
   fails, creates harm cycles, doesn't address root causes.
   *(source-paraphrase)*

4. **Restorative alternatives.** Restorative justice and therapeutic
   approaches are more effective than punitive ones — they address
   understanding and motivation, not just behavior. *(source-paraphrase)*

5. **AGI design implications.** AGI should use pure information signals
   for motivation, not pain or punishment mechanisms. *(source-paraphrase)*

6. **Evolutionary transcendence.** Pain and punishment are adaptations for
   limited cognition; sufficient intelligence transcends both.
   *(source-paraphrase)*

7. **Ethical imperative.** Preserving suffering when alternatives exist
   is ethically unjustifiable — applies to both human treatment and
   AGI design. *(source-paraphrase)*

### Hyperseed-ontology claims

8. **Pain = crude fiber signal.** Pain uses a one-dimensional pain fiber
   (intensity only) to encode what could be a multi-dimensional information
   fiber (type, severity, appropriate response). *(hyperseed-interpretation)*

9. **Pain fiber vs. information fiber.** dim(F_pain) = 1 (cheap, attention-
   grabbing, informationally poor) vs. dim(F_info) ≫ 1 (costly, requires
   sophistication, informationally rich). *(hyperseed-interpretation)*

10. **Punishment = base-space coercion.** Punishment constrains base-space
    movement without changing fiber structure (understanding/motivation).
    *(hyperseed-interpretation)*

11. **Restorative = fiber-level intervention.** Restorative approaches
    reshape agent fiber structure — change understanding and capability,
    not just behavior constraints. *(hyperseed-interpretation)*

12. **Coercion-understanding asymmetry.** Base-space coercion has diminishing
    returns; fiber-level understanding has increasing returns.
    *(hyperseed-interpretation)*

13. **Pain gradient degeneracy (d-calculus).** Pain provides rank-1 gradient;
    information fiber provides full-rank ∇_info ∈ R^n. Pain's covariant
    derivative is degenerate. *(hyperseed-interpretation)*

14. **Punishment curvature (d-calculus).** High curvature of punishment
    connection means effectiveness varies wildly with context — why
    one-size-fits-all punishment fails. *(hyperseed-interpretation)*

15. **Restorative holonomy (d-calculus).** Restorative cycle produces non-
    trivial holonomy (genuine change); punitive cycle produces trivial
    holonomy or regression. *(hyperseed-interpretation)*

16. **Evolutionary transcendence as fiber upgrade (d-calculus).** Transcending
    pain = replacing F_pain ≅ R with F_info ≅ R^n — enrichment, not
    removal. *(hyperseed-interpretation)*

17. **AGI flat information connection (d-calculus).** AGI motivation should
    use flat connection — undistorted gradient flow toward goals. No pain
    curvature, no punishment coercion. *(hyperseed-interpretation)*
