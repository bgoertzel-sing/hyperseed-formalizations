\begin{abstract}
We formalize Ben Goertzel's ``The Architecture of Collective Non-Self''
(2026-03-26) within the Hyperseed ontology, incorporating d-calculus
extensions from Hyperseed v2. Location Zero organizations are modeled as
rigid ego fiber with high boundary curvature, open-source architecture
as dissolved fiber boundaries with near-zero fork curvature, and
collective consciousness as an emergent topological property measured
by the emergence gap curvature.
\end{abstract}

\section{Location Zero: rigid ego fiber}

\begin{definition}[Ego fiber --- claims 1, 6]
A \emph{Location Zero organization} has rigid ego fiber:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Self-model: a strong fiber section $\sigma_{\text{self}}$ that
    prioritizes organizational survival.
  \item Rigid boundaries: sharp inside/outside distinction with controlled
    information transport across the boundary.
  \item Self-preservation connection: $\Gamma_{\text{ego}}$ prioritizes
    transport that serves survival; suppresses threatening information.
\end{itemize}
\end{definition}

\section{Location 1+: dissolved fiber boundary}

\begin{definition}[Non-self architecture --- claims 2, 4, 7]
An \emph{open-source architecture} produces dissolved fiber boundaries:
\begin{itemize}
  \item Fork-ability: the fiber bundle can be replicated arbitrarily.
    No scarcity drives boundary rigidity.
  \item Transparency: all fiber is visible. No hidden fiber requires
    boundary protection.
  \item Voluntary connection: $\Gamma_{\text{voluntary}}$ is non-coercive ---
    participants connect freely, not by contract.
  \item No self-preservation: the project has no existential attachment
    to its own continuation.
\end{itemize}
\end{definition}

\section{Emergent consciousness as fiber topology}

\begin{proposition}[Topology determines consciousness --- claims 3, 5, 8]
\label{prop:topology}
The collective consciousness ``location'' is a topological property of
the organizational fiber bundle:
\[
  \text{Location}(\text{org}) = f(\text{topology}(E_{\text{org}}))
\]
It depends on how fiber connects (topology), not on what the fiber
contains (individual beliefs/motivations). An organization of ego-driven
individuals can exhibit non-ego collective behavior if the topology
is right (fork-able, transparent, voluntary).
\end{proposition}

\section{No privileged section: non-self}
\label{sec:nonself}
%% =================================================================

\begin{definition}[Non-self quality --- claims 15--19, 26]
\label{def:nonself}
OSS projects have a ``non-self'' quality that resonates with Buddhist
anatta: no fixed institutional identity, no rigid boundary, no
self-preservation instinct. The project exists as a process, not a thing.

In Hyperseed terms, ``non-self'' maps to \emph{no privileged section}:
there is no fixed, permanent, authoritative perspective on what the
project ``really is.'' Every section (version, fork, interpretation)
is contingent, temporary, and replaceable:
\[
  \nexists\, s^* \in \Gamma(E) \text{ such that } s^*
  \text{ is ``the real'' section}
\]

The bundle $E$ exists --- the project has structure, history, community
--- but no section $s^*$ is privileged. This is the organizational analog
of anatta: the self (privileged section) is an illusion. What exists is
the bundle (the project-as-process), not any particular section
(the project-as-fixed-identity).

When an OSS project dies, it's a \emph{fiber endpoint without bundle
collapse}: one particular fiber (the active development community)
terminates, but the bundle persists in forks, derivatives, and the
ecosystem's collective codebase. Death is local (one fiber ends) not
global (the bundle collapses). This is why OSS project death isn't
tragic in the way corporate bankruptcy is: the bundle survives even
when particular fibers don't.
\end{definition}

\section{Fiber engineering}
\label{sec:engineering}
%% =================================================================

\begin{proposition}[Architecture as path --- claims 20--22, 27]
\label{prop:engineering}
The organizational architecture is itself a path to collective higher
consciousness --- a structural rather than psychological or spiritual
path. Unlike individual enlightenment (personal practice, individual
psychology), architectural transcendence is replicable and scalable.

In Hyperseed terms, this is \emph{fiber engineering}: designing the
fiber topology (fork/merge structure, boundary permeability,
transparency) to produce desired emergent properties. The engineering
operates on the architecture (fiber topology), not on individual
participants (fiber content):
\[
  \text{Design } T_{\text{bundle}} \implies
  \text{emergent properties } P(T_{\text{bundle}})
\]

This is replicable because it's structural: given the same topology,
the same emergent properties appear regardless of who the individual
participants are. It's scalable because topology scales: the same
fork/merge/transparency principles work for small projects and massive
ones.

The implication for AGI governance: if organizational architecture
produces collective consciousness level, then designing AGI governance
architectures is a form of fiber engineering --- designing the topology
to produce the desired collective behavior (beneficial AGI development)
regardless of individual participant motivations.
\end{proposition}

\begin{conjecture}[Architectural consciousness transfer]
Conjecture: the consciousness-raising properties of open-source
architecture are transferable to other domains --- not just software
but any organizational endeavor that can be made forkable, transparent,
voluntarily participated in, and free from institutional self-preservation.

If this conjecture holds, the principles of OSS architecture could be
applied to scientific research (open science), governance (open governance),
education (open education), and AI development (open AGI) to produce
Location 1+ collective behavior in each domain. The key is not the
specific domain but the topological properties: fork-ability, permeability,
transparency, voluntary participation, and absence of institutional
self-preservation.

The limiting factor is not technical but structural: can the domain
support fork-ability? Software can be forked trivially; physical
infrastructure cannot. The transferability depends on how ``forkable''
the domain's core assets are.
\end{conjecture}

\section{d-calculus formulation (Hyperseed v2)}

\begin{proposition}[Ego boundary curvature --- claim 9]
\label{prop:ego}
The curvature at the organizational boundary measures ego rigidity:
\[
  \|F_{\nabla_{\text{boundary}}}\| = \text{organizational ego rigidity}
\]
\begin{itemize}
  \item Location Zero: $\|F_{\nabla_{\text{boundary}}}\| \gg 1$
    (rigid ego, sharp inside/outside).
  \item Location 1+: $\|F_{\nabla_{\text{boundary}}}\| \approx 0$
    (dissolved ego, permeable boundary).
\end{itemize}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Fork-ability curvature --- claim 10]
\label{prop:fork}
The curvature of the fork operation:
\[
  \|F_{\nabla_{\text{fork}}}\| = \text{difficulty of forking}
\]
\begin{itemize}
  \item Open-source: $\|F_{\nabla_{\text{fork}}}\| \approx 0$
    (trivial to fork --- one command).
  \item Proprietary: $\|F_{\nabla_{\text{fork}}}\| \to \infty$
    (legally and practically impossible).
\end{itemize}
Low fork curvature is the structural foundation of non-self: when
the project can be freely replicated, there is nothing unique to
preserve, and self-preservation loses its grip.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Non-self holonomy --- claim 11]
\label{prop:holonomy}
The organizational lifecycle loop $\gamma$: create $\to$ grow $\to$
fork $\to$ merge $\to$ transform produces holonomy:
\[
  \operatorname{Hol}_\gamma(\Gamma_{\text{org}}) \neq \mathrm{id}
  \implies \text{organization changes through lifecycle}
\]
\begin{itemize}
  \item High holonomy + graceful transformation: non-self (Location 1+).
    The organization embraces change without existential crisis.
  \item Low holonomy + resistance: ego (Location Zero). The organization
    resists change to preserve identity.
\end{itemize}
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Architecture-as-path gradient --- claim 12]
\label{prop:gradient}
The gradient from Location Zero to Location 1+:
\[
  \nabla_{\text{location}} = \nabla\left(\text{fork-ability} \times
    \text{transparency} \times \text{voluntary} \times
    (1 - \text{self-preservation})\right)
\]
Open-source architecture naturally follows this gradient. The
architectural choices (made for practical reasons: collaboration, code
sharing) move the organization toward non-self without requiring
individual spiritual development.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proposition}[Emergent consciousness curvature --- claim 13]
\label{prop:emergence}
The curvature between individual and collective consciousness:
\[
  \|F_\nabla(\text{individual}, \text{collective})\| =
    \text{emergence gap}
\]
High emergence gap: collective consciousness differs greatly from
individual --- the whole is genuinely more than the sum. Open-source
architecture produces high emergence gap: Location 1+ collective
behavior from Location Zero individuals.
\end{proposition}
