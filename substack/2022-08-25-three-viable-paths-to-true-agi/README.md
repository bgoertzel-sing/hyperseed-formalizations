## Summary

Goertzel outlines three viable paths to achieving true AGI, arguing that
each has genuine potential but different risk profiles, timelines, and
resource requirements. He advocates for pursuing all three in parallel,
with special emphasis on the hybrid neural-symbolic approach embodied
by OpenCog Hyperon.

### Core argument

1. **Hybrid neural-symbolic.** Combining neural learning with symbolic
   reasoning in integrated architectures (OpenCog Hyperon). Neural nets
   provide perception, pattern recognition, and fuzzy generalization;
   symbolic systems provide explicit reasoning, planning, and knowledge
   representation. The integration (cognitive synergy) produces capabilities
   neither has alone.

2. **Brain emulation.** Whole brain emulation — scanning and simulating
   brain structure at sufficient detail to reproduce cognitive function.
   This path requires advances in neuroscience (understanding what level
   of detail matters), scanning technology (resolution and speed), and
   computation (simulating the scanned structure efficiently).

3. **Evolutionary/developmental.** Growing AGI through evolutionary and
   developmental processes — artificial evolution creating diverse
   cognitive architectures, developmental learning building complexity
   from simple beginnings, embodied interaction grounding cognition in
   physical reality.

4. **Complementary, not exclusive.** The three paths are complementary:
   - Brain emulation informs hybrid design (what architecture works).
   - Evolution discovers novel architectures neither designed nor copied.
   - Hybrid systems provide interpretable testbeds for evolutionary output.
   Insights from each path feed the others.

5. **Risk profiles differ.** Each path has distinct risks:
   - Hybrid: design errors, integration failures, incomplete cognitive
     synergy.
   - Emulation: unknown computational requirements, scan resolution
     uncertainties, "zombie" risk (functional without conscious).
   - Evolution: unpredictable outcomes, alignment difficulties with
     evolved systems, long timelines.

6. **Scaling ≠ AGI.** Scaling current LLM architectures alone is not a
   path to true AGI (see "Closed-Ended Quasi-AGI"). The three paths all
   involve qualitative architectural innovations, not just quantitative
   scaling.

7. **Resource allocation.** Current AI funding is overwhelmingly directed
   at scaling LLMs (not a viable path) rather than these three viable
   paths. A rational allocation would fund all three, with emphasis on
   hybrid (most mature, best understood) and smaller allocations to
   emulation and evolution.

### Connection to other Goertzel work

- **General Theory of GI:** Provides theoretical grounding for why hybrid
  approaches work — cognitive synergy as the mechanism of general intelligence.
- **Closed-Ended Quasi-AGI:** Explains why LLM scaling is not a fourth path.
- **Consciousness explosion:** Each path leads to different types of
  consciousness — biological-like (emulation), designed (hybrid), evolved
  (evolutionary). The consciousness explosion includes all three.
- **OpenCog/Hyperon:** Direct implementation of path 1.

## Hyperseed ontology interpretation

### Three fiber construction methods

Each path is a distinct method for constructing the fiber bundle that
constitutes AGI:

- **Hybrid construction (designed fiber):** The fiber bundle is
  explicitly constructed by composing different fiber types — neural
  fibers (perception, pattern matching), symbolic fibers (reasoning,
  planning), memory fibers, action fibers. The composition is guided
  by architectural design. The challenge is ensuring the composed
  bundle has emergent properties (cognitive synergy) not present in
  any component fiber.

- **Emulation construction (copied fiber):** The fiber bundle is copied
  from an existing biological system. The original biological fiber
  bundle (a human brain) is scanned at sufficient resolution and
  replicated in computational substrate. The challenge is determining
  which aspects of the biological fiber are essential (structural fiber
  vs. substrate artifact) and reproducing them faithfully.

- **Evolutionary construction (evolved fiber):** The fiber bundle
  emerges through evolutionary selection. Starting from simple initial
  fiber structures, variation (mutation, crossover) produces diverse
  fiber architectures, and selection (fitness evaluation) retains those
  that exhibit intelligence. The challenge is that evolution is slow
  and its products unpredictable — the evolved fiber may be functionally
  intelligent but structurally incomprehensible.

### Complementarity as fiber cross-pollination

The complementarity of the three paths is fiber cross-pollination:

- **Emulation → Hybrid:** Scanned biological fiber reveals which fiber
  compositions work in nature — informing hybrid design. The emulated
  bundle provides a "reference fiber" against which designed bundles
  can be compared.

- **Evolution → Hybrid:** Evolved fiber discovers novel compositions
  that no designer would propose. These can be analyzed, understood,
  and incorporated into hybrid designs.

- **Hybrid → Evolution:** Hybrid systems provide interpretable fitness
  landscapes for evolutionary search — fitness can be evaluated on
  specific cognitive tasks rather than brute survival.

### d-calculus connection (Hyperseed v2)

- **Construction curvature.** Each construction method introduces
  characteristic curvature patterns in the resulting fiber bundle:
  - Designed fiber: curvature concentrated at interface boundaries
    between fiber types (neural-symbolic junctions). The challenge is
    minimizing interface curvature.
  - Copied fiber: curvature pattern reproduces biological curvature
    (which evolution has smoothed over millions of years). The challenge
    is reproducing the curvature faithfully during scanning/simulation.
  - Evolved fiber: curvature pattern is unpredictable — evolution finds
    solutions that work regardless of curvature aesthetics. May have
    high curvature that is nonetheless functionally effective.

- **Fiber growth dynamics.** Each path has different fiber growth
  dynamics (∂F/∂t):
  - Hybrid: growth is designed and modular — new fiber types can be
    added deliberately. Growth rate controllable.
  - Emulation: growth replicates biological growth dynamics — neural
    plasticity, synaptic modification. Growth rate matches biology
    (unless accelerated).
  - Evolution: growth is generational — each generation produces new
    fiber architectures. Growth rate is evolutionary (slow per
    generation, but parallelizable).

- **Holonomy signatures.** Each construction method produces
  characteristic holonomy patterns:
  - Designed: holonomy reflects architectural choices — which fiber
    components interact, and how.
  - Copied: holonomy mirrors biological holonomy — the developmental
    history of the copied brain.
  - Evolved: holonomy reflects evolutionary history — the selection
    pressures that shaped the fiber.
  These holonomy signatures are "fingerprints" that identify how an
  AGI's fiber bundle was constructed.

- **Scaling curvature divergence.** The reason LLM scaling doesn't
  reach AGI has a curvature interpretation: scaling increases fiber
  volume (more parameters = more fiber capacity) without changing
  fiber curvature (the architecture determines curvature, not the
  scale). AGI requires specific curvature patterns (cognitive synergy
  curvature, self-modification holonomy, etc.) that don't emerge from
  increasing the volume of a fixed-curvature architecture.
