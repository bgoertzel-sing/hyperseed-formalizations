# Claim inventory — We Should Be Measuring Well-Being

Source: Ben Goertzel, "We Should Be Measuring Well-Being Catalysis,
Not (trying and failing to) Measure GDP," Eurykosmotron, 2022-10-10.

### Core claims from the article

1. **GDP measures activity, not well-being.** GDP counts economic activity
   regardless of contribution to flourishing — pollution cleanup, preventable
   disease spending count; unpaid care, leisure don't. GDP can rise while
   well-being falls. *(source-paraphrase)*

2. **Hedonic adjustment failures.** Hedonic quality adjustments are ad hoc
   and inadequate; the gap between GDP and what matters widens as technology
   transforms what products are. *(source-paraphrase)*

3. **Well-being metrics exist.** Gross National Happiness, OECD Better Life
   Index, World Happiness Report, and subjective well-being surveys capture
   dimensions GDP misses. *(source-paraphrase)*

4. **Measurement shapes policy.** What you measure is what you optimize.
   Measuring GDP → optimize growth regardless of well-being effects.
   Measuring well-being → optimize genuine flourishing. *(source-paraphrase)*

5. **AGI makes GDP meaningless.** As AGI automates production, GDP disconnects
   entirely from human welfare. Post-AGI GDP could be astronomical with
   everyone miserable. Only well-being metrics capture what matters.
   *(source-paraphrase)*

6. **Consciousness connection.** Ultimate well-being measurement requires
   understanding consciousness — what it means for a being to genuinely
   flourish, not just what surveys report. *(source-paraphrase)*

7. **Well-being catalysis.** Measuring the factors that catalyze well-being
   may be more measurable and actionable than measuring well-being itself.
   *(source-paraphrase)*
