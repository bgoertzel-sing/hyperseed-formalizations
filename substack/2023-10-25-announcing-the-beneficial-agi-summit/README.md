## Summary

Goertzel announces the BGI Summit Unconference in Panama City (Feb 27–Mar 1,
2024), bringing together diverse AGI researchers and stakeholders to discuss
paths toward beneficial AGI. The article articulates the philosophical and
practical case for open, decentralized, diverse approaches to AGI development.

### Core argument

1. **Open collaboration.** AGI development benefits from open, diverse
   collaboration across approaches rather than siloed corporate efforts.
   The most important problems require diverse perspectives that no
   single organization possesses.

2. **Beneficial focus.** The summit focuses specifically on *beneficial*
   AGI — not just capability but ensuring AGI development serves broad
   human (and non-human) interests. Capability without beneficence is
   dangerous; beneficence without capability is ineffective.

3. **Decentralized governance.** AGI governance should be decentralized
   and participatory, not top-down regulatory capture by incumbents.
   Centralized control of AGI concentrates unprecedented power; only
   distributed governance can prevent this.

4. **Unconference format.** The unconference format embodies the open,
   emergent, participatory principles the BGI movement advocates.
   Structure emerges from participant interaction rather than top-down
   programming. This mirrors the cognitive synergy principle — diverse
   agents interacting produce emergent intelligence.

5. **Diverse approaches essential.** No single approach to AGI is
   guaranteed to succeed or to be safe. Portfolio diversity across
   approaches (hybrid, emulation, evolutionary, neurosymbolic) is both
   a research strategy and a safety strategy.

6. **Community building.** The summit builds community — personal
   connections between researchers who might otherwise compete. Trust
   networks are essential for navigating the AGI transition safely.

7. **Counterweight to corporate AGI.** The BGI movement provides a
   counterweight to corporate AGI development, which optimizes for
   profit rather than benefit. Independent, open-source, community-
   governed AGI ensures competitive pressure for beneficence.

### Connection to other Goertzel work

- **Decentralization:** The summit is decentralized governance in action
  — participatory, open, emergent.
- **Three Viable Paths:** The summit brings together practitioners of
  all three paths (and others) for cross-pollination.
- **Singularitarian Politics:** The BGI movement is a practical
  expression of singularitarian political organization.
- **Well-being Measurement:** Beneficial AGI requires measuring
  benefit — connecting to well-being metrics.

## Hyperseed ontology interpretation

### Summit as fiber cross-pollination event

In the Hyperseed framework, the summit is a fiber cross-pollination
event — a temporary space where normally disconnected fiber bundles
(different research groups, approaches, perspectives) are brought into
contact:

- **Normal state:** Research groups develop fiber bundles independently.
  Each has its own base space (research context), fiber structure
  (theoretical framework), and connection (methodology). They share
  a global base (the AGI problem) but have disconnected fibers.

- **Summit state:** The summit creates a temporary shared base space
  where fibers from different groups overlap. Fiber contact enables:
  - Cross-pollination: fiber elements from one bundle transfer to
    another (ideas shared between groups).
  - Compatibility testing: fibers from different bundles are compared
    to check section compatibility (do the theories agree where they
    overlap?).
  - Emergent fiber: new fiber emerges at contact points that didn't
    exist in any individual bundle (new ideas from interaction).

### Unconference as emergent fiber structure

The unconference format produces emergent fiber — structure that arises
from participant interaction rather than top-down imposition:

- **Imposed structure:** Traditional conferences have pre-determined
  fiber: fixed schedule, invited talks, imposed topic categories.
  The fiber structure is designed by organizers and participants
  navigate within it.

- **Emergent structure:** Unconferences let fiber structure emerge from
  participant interaction. Topics, groups, and connections form
  spontaneously based on actual interest and expertise. The resulting
  fiber is better adapted to the participants' actual fiber structure
  than any pre-designed structure could be.

### Beneficial AGI as fiber ethics

"Beneficial" is a constraint on fiber evolution — not any fiber growth
is acceptable, only fiber growth that increases collective flourishing:

  dF/dt > 0 AND d(flourishing)/dt > 0

This couples fiber growth (capability) to flourishing growth (benefit).
The coupling is the ethical constraint that distinguishes beneficial AGI
from arbitrary AGI.

### d-calculus connection (Hyperseed v2)

- **Cross-pollination curvature.** The curvature at fiber contact points
  measures the difficulty of cross-pollination. Low curvature = easy
  transfer (compatible frameworks); high curvature = difficult transfer
  (incompatible frameworks, but potentially more valuable if achieved).

- **Emergent fiber gradient.** The emergent fiber at the summit has a
  gradient ∇F_emergent that points in directions no individual group
  was pursuing. This gradient represents genuinely new research
  directions that arise only from collective interaction — the
  cognitive synergy of the research community.

- **Trust as connection coefficient.** The personal trust built at the
  summit is a connection coefficient Γ_trust in the social fiber
  bundle. Trust enables parallel transport of sensitive information
  (safety concerns, alignment strategies) that can't traverse
  low-trust connections. Building trust = building connection.

- **Governance holonomy.** Decentralized governance cycles produce
  holonomy in the governance fiber bundle. Each summit cycle (plan →
  meet → discuss → decide → implement → evaluate) produces non-trivial
  holonomy — the governance structure evolves with each cycle, adapting
  to new information and changing conditions.

- **Portfolio curvature.** The curvature across the portfolio of AGI
  approaches measures diversification benefit. Low total curvature =
  approaches are too similar (concentrated risk). High total curvature =
  approaches are genuinely diverse (distributed risk). The summit
  maximizes portfolio curvature by bringing together maximally diverse
  approaches.
