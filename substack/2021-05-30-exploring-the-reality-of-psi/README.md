## Summary

Explores psi (paranormal) phenomena — anomalous perception and causation —
from the perspective of a scientifically-minded person who has witnessed
convincing demonstrations. Argues that psi is real but poorly understood,
and that future science (possibly involving AGI) may provide explanations.

### Core argument

1. **Personal experience.** First-hand witnessing of psi demonstrations
   that resist conventional explanation — not anecdotal hearsay but
   direct observation by a trained scientist.

2. **Statistical evidence.** Meta-analyses of psi experiments (Ganzfeld,
   remote viewing, presentiment) show small but consistent effects that
   are statistically significant. The effect sizes are small but the
   consistency across studies and experimenters is notable.

3. **Physics speculation.** Psi might involve non-local correlations
   (entanglement-like effects at macro scale), retrocausal influences,
   or physics beyond the Standard Model. Current physics doesn't
   explain psi but also doesn't definitively rule it out.

4. **Consciousness connection.** Psi may be deeply connected to
   consciousness — both involve aspects of reality not well captured
   by current physics. The "hard problem" of consciousness and the
   "hard problem" of psi may be related.

5. **Future science.** AGI and future physics may provide frameworks
   for understanding psi that current science lacks. An AGI that can
   reason across disciplines might see connections humans miss.

6. **Scientific taboo.** Psi research is stigmatized in mainstream
   science — not because the evidence is weak but because the
   conclusions are uncomfortable. This is a sociological problem,
   not an epistemic one.

### Connection to other Goertzel work

- **World crystal:** If reality is fundamentally computational/discrete,
  psi might be information leakage between normally separated regions
  of the computational substrate.
- **Consciousness explosion:** Psi capabilities might expand with
  enhanced consciousness — more conscious beings might have stronger
  psi effects.
- **Paraconsistency:** Psi phenomena may require paraconsistent logic
  to describe — they violate classical assumptions about locality and
  causality without being "impossible."

## Hyperseed ontology interpretation

### Psi as non-local fiber correlation

In the Hyperseed framework, psi phenomena involve fiber correlations
that don't respect base-space locality:

- **Local fiber correlation (normal).** In normal cognition, fibers
  at nearby base points are correlated through local connections —
  adjacent neurons, nearby spatial locations, temporally close events.
  The connection Γ mediates correlation only between adjacent base
  points.

- **Non-local fiber correlation (psi).** Psi phenomena suggest fibers
  at distant base points (spatially or temporally separated) are
  correlated without any local connection path. The correlation
  bypasses the base-space structure entirely.

- **Fiber shortcut vs. hidden connection.** Two interpretations:
  1. Genuine non-locality: fiber correlations that cannot be mediated
     by any base-space path (true psi = fiber correlation without
     base-space support).
  2. Hidden connection: base-space connections exist but are invisible
     to current physics (psi = fiber correlation through hidden
     base-space dimensions).

### Consciousness as psi enabler

Consciousness may be the fiber property that enables non-local
correlation:

- **Conscious fiber = self-referential fiber.** Consciousness involves
  fiber that models itself — fiber sections that include a model of
  the fiber bundle they belong to. This self-reference may create
  loops that bypass base-space locality.

- **Psi strength ∝ consciousness intensity.** If consciousness enables
  non-local fiber correlation, then more intense consciousness should
  produce stronger psi effects. This aligns with reports that psi is
  enhanced by meditation, altered states, and focused attention.

### d-calculus connection (Hyperseed v2)

- **Non-local connection.** In standard fiber bundle theory, connection
  is local — Γ(b) depends only on the base point b and its
  infinitesimal neighborhood. Psi requires a non-local connection:

  Γ_psi(b₁, b₂) mediates correlation between distant points b₁, b₂

  This violates the locality axiom of standard differential geometry.
  The d-calculus extension for psi needs a generalized connection that
  includes non-local terms.

- **Non-local curvature.** The curvature of the psi connection measures
  the strength and geometry of non-local correlations:

  F_psi(b₁, b₂) = strength of non-local correlation between b₁ and b₂

  Non-zero F_psi between distant points = psi effect. The curvature
  decays with distance in normal physics (F → 0 as d(b₁,b₂) → ∞)
  but psi suggests non-zero F_psi at arbitrary distances.

- **Retrocausal holonomy.** Retrocausal psi (precognition, presentiment)
  involves holonomy around temporal loops:

  Hol_γ(Γ_psi) ≠ id where γ traverses t_future → t_present

  The temporal loop produces non-trivial holonomy — information from
  the future affects the present fiber. This is only consistent if
  the base space includes temporal non-locality (closed timelike
  curves in the fiber bundle base, or equivalently, retrocausal
  connections in the fiber connection).

- **Statistical curvature.** The small but consistent statistical
  effects in psi experiments correspond to small but non-zero
  curvature:

  ||F_psi|| ≈ ε ≪ 1, but ε > 0 consistently across experiments

  The effect is small (small curvature) but robust (non-zero in
  every careful experiment). This is analogous to a weak but
  persistent field — small curvature that nonetheless has measurable
  effects when integrated over many measurements.

- **Taboo as connection suppression.** Scientific stigma against psi
  is modeled as connection suppression in the sociological fiber
  bundle — the trust connection Γ_trust between psi researchers and
  mainstream science is suppressed, preventing parallel transport of
  psi evidence into mainstream fiber.
