## Summary

Goertzel argues that the singularitarian/transhumanist movement needs
organized political action — a serious political party or equivalent
organization — to influence policy during the critical window before
AGI arrives.

### Core argument

1. **Political organization needed.** Singularitarians and transhumanists
   hold transformative ideas about the future but lack organized political
   representation. Individual advocacy is insufficient; a coherent
   political movement is needed to influence policy effectively.

2. **Key policy positions.** A singularitarian party would advocate:
   - Massive public funding for AGI research, life extension, and space.
   - Opposition to overly restrictive AI regulation that would slow
     beneficial AGI development.
   - Support for open science and open-source AI.
   - Universal basic income and post-scarcity economic transition planning.
   - Radical life extension research funding.
   - Cognitive liberty — the right to modify one's own mind.

3. **Beyond left-right.** Singularitarianism cuts across traditional
   political divisions. It's neither left nor right but orthogonal —
   a new political dimension centered on the future of intelligence
   and consciousness rather than traditional economic or cultural issues.

4. **Democratic engagement.** The approach should be democratic — making
   the public case for singularitarian policies, not imposing them
   technocratically. Persuasion, education, and democratic participation,
   not elitist imposition.

5. **Urgency.** The window for beneficial political influence on AGI
   development is limited. Once AGI arrives, the political landscape
   may be fundamentally transformed. Organizing now is essential for
   ensuring beneficial outcomes.

6. **Global perspective.** The party/movement should be global, not
   national — AGI affects everyone, and national-level politics alone
   is insufficient for a global transformation.

7. **Coalition building.** The movement should build coalitions with
   existing progressive, libertarian, and futurist movements where
   interests align, rather than insisting on ideological purity.

### Connection to other Goertzel work

- **Decentralization:** A singularitarian party embodies decentralized
  governance principles — DAOs, liquid democracy, quadratic voting.
- **Consciousness explosion:** The party's mission is to ensure the
  consciousness explosion is beneficial and widely shared.
- **Well-being measurement:** The party would use well-being metrics
  (not GDP) to evaluate policy success.
- **Beneficial AGI:** The party is the political arm of the beneficial
  AGI movement.

## Hyperseed ontology interpretation

### Political organization as fiber coordination

In the Hyperseed framework, political organization is the coordination
of distributed individual fibers into a coherent collective bundle:

- **Unorganized state:** Individual singularitarians are thin, scattered
  fibers — each holds transformative beliefs but has negligible
  political fiber (influence). F_individual(b_politics) ≈ 0 for each.

- **Organized state:** A political party bundles individual fibers into
  a thick, coherent collective fiber:
  
  F_party = bundle(F_1, F_2, ..., F_n)
  
  The bundle has political fiber at base points where individuals had
  none. Collective action produces emergent political capability that
  no individual possesses.

### Orthogonal political fiber

Singularitarianism as a political dimension orthogonal to left-right:

  F_singularitarian ⊥ F_left-right

This is a genuine new fiber dimension in political space — not a position
on the existing axis but the opening of a new axis. Policy positions
(fund AGI, life extension, cognitive liberty) don't map onto the
traditional spectrum because they concern a dimension (the future of
intelligence) that the traditional spectrum doesn't address.

### Fiber window = pre-transition organizing

The urgency claim maps to a closing fiber window:

  t < t_AGI → organize fiber now
  t ≥ t_AGI → fiber structure may be determined

Before the AGI phase transition, the political fiber bundle is plastic —
it can be shaped by organized action. After the transition, the fiber
structure may be locked in (for better or worse). Pre-transition fiber
coordination is essential for beneficial outcomes.

### d-calculus connection (Hyperseed v2)

- **Coalition curvature:** Building coalitions across political traditions
  involves navigating curvature in political fiber space. The curvature
  between singularitarian fiber and existing political fibers determines
  the "coalition cost" — how much compromise is needed to achieve
  alignment. Low curvature = easy coalition; high curvature = difficult
  but potentially more powerful if achieved.

- **Democratic holonomy:** Democratic governance cycles (propose → debate →
  vote → implement → evaluate → propose) produce holonomy in the
  political fiber bundle. Non-trivial holonomy = genuine democratic
  evolution; trivial = mere cycling. A well-functioning singularitarian
  party produces non-trivial holonomy with each governance cycle.

- **Urgency as curvature acceleration:** The narrowing fiber window
  corresponds to increasing curvature in the time direction:
  
  d²F/dt² > 0 → curvature accelerating → window closing faster
  
  The political landscape curves more sharply as AGI approaches,
  making organized action both more difficult and more important.

- **Global fiber = base-space coverage:** A global movement has fiber
  coverage over the full geopolitical base space B_geo, not just a
  national subregion. Global coverage ensures the fiber structure
  is robust to national-level perturbations.
