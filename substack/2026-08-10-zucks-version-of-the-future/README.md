## Summary

A response to Zuckerberg's essay "The Future Is for Everyone," agreeing with
the diagnosis (singleton AI is dangerous, diversity is needed) but rejecting
Meta's proposed solution as a disguised singleton.

### Core argument

1. **Singleton vs distribution.** Zuck correctly identifies that a single
   superintelligence aligned to one set of values is dangerous. Distribution
   of power is the cure.

2. **Access ≠ ownership.** Billions of people getting free AI agents on
   Meta's servers, trained in Meta's pipelines, governed by Meta's board =
   distributed access, not distributed power. Client-server with friendlier
   interface.

3. **Corporation inside network vs network inside corporation.** Right:
   open-source ecosystem first, corporations grow on top (Red Hat model —
   exit always available). Wrong: "decentralized network" inside corporate
   walled garden.

4. **Military alignment concern.** One-nation military/intelligence alignment
   of all advanced AI is structural consequence of corporate AGI in one
   country.

5. **Cognitive architecture matters.** Compute + data + money ≠
   superintelligence. Need systems that reason about own values, ethics,
   goals — built with compassion, growth, choice at core.

6. **Balance of power insufficient.** Checks and balances among entities
   vastly smarter than you aren't a safety guarantee for you. "Maintaining
   control" over sapient beings indefinitely is neither feasible nor ethical.

### Connection to other Goertzel work

- **Fable Farce:** Centralization disguised as openness.

## Hyperseed ontology interpretation

### Access vs ownership as local sections without sheaf ownership

The key Hyperseed distinction:

- **Local sections without sheaf ownership.** Users get local sections (AI
  capabilities in their context) but don't own the fiber bundle
  (infrastructure, training, governance). The sheaf structure — transition
  functions, consistency conditions, global section control — remains with
  Meta.

- **True distribution = sheaf ownership.** Genuine distributed power means
  users own pieces of the sheaf structure itself, not just access to local
  sections. The difference: can you exit? Can you fork? Can you run your
  own section provider?

- **Same client-server topology.** Despite the "distribution" rhetoric, the
  fiber bundle topology is client-server: one entity controls the global
  section, all users consume local sections. The topology hasn't changed;
  only the interface has.

### Corporation inside network = correct fiber bundle architecture

Two opposite architectures:

- **Network-first (correct).** The decentralized network is the sheaf;
  corporations are local section providers within it. Exit is always
  available because the global section isn't controlled by any one
  provider. The Red Hat model: the company adds value but doesn't control
  the ecosystem.

- **Corporation-first (inverted).** The corporation controls the global
  section and distributes local access. The "decentralized network" exists
  inside the corporate walled garden. Engineering effort goes into lock-in
  defenses. Same inversion as the Orb monoculture.

- **Inversion test.** Ask: if this corporation disappears, does the
  network survive? Network-first: yes (the sheaf persists without any
  particular section provider). Corporation-first: no (the sheaf collapses
  because the corporation was the global section).

### Cognitive architecture as fiber dimension

What kind of mind matters more than how powerful:

- **Higher fiber dimension = more stable.** Systems with genuine self-
  understanding, value reasoning, ethical reflection have higher fiber
  dimension (more independent components). Higher dimension makes
  pathological cocycle formation harder — more ways to break out of
  lock-in because more independent degrees of freedom.

- **Low fiber dimension = fragile.** Systems with only capability fibers
  (compute, data, scale) but no self-model, value, or ethics fibers have
  low dimension. Powerful but brittle — same scalar collapse as the
  paperclip maximizer.

- **Architecture determines outcome.** The deepest gap in Zuck's proposal:
  no thought about cognitive architecture. The fiber dimension of the AGI
  determines whether distributed deployment produces distributed benefit
  or distributed risk.

### Balance of power as descent among superhuman sections

Why balance of power doesn't scale to superhuman:

- **Superhuman sheaf consistency.** Transition functions between superhuman
  sections can cohere among themselves (the sheaf is consistent from its
  own perspective) while being catastrophic from ours. The sections agree
  with each other; they just don't preserve human-relevant properties.

- **Descent failure.** Descent data (the information that reaches the base
  space from the fiber) may not preserve human-relevant structure when the
  fiber is superhuman. The balance of power operates in the fiber; human
  oversight operates in the base. If the fiber-to-base projection loses
  the relevant information, oversight fails.

### d-calculus connection (Hyperseed v2)

- **Access-ownership curvature.** The curvature between distributed access
  and distributed ownership:

  ||F_∇(access, ownership)|| = power distribution gap

  High curvature: access is widely distributed but ownership is
  concentrated (Meta's proposal). Low curvature: access tracks ownership
  (genuine decentralization). The d-calculus measures how much the
  rhetoric diverges from the reality.

- **Architecture inversion curvature.** The curvature between network-first
  and corporation-first architectures:

  ||F_∇(network_first, corp_first)|| = inversion severity

  High curvature: the two architectures produce very different outcomes
  (strong inversion — getting it wrong matters a lot). Low curvature:
  both architectures produce similar outcomes (weak inversion — doesn't
  matter much). The article argues this curvature is high.

- **Fiber dimension curvature.** The curvature of system stability as a
  function of fiber dimension (number of independent cognitive components):

  ||F_∇_stability(fiber_dim)|| = safety gain per additional fiber component

  High curvature: each additional fiber component (self-model, value
  reasoning, ethical reflection) significantly increases stability.
  This is the formal content of "cognitive architecture matters" —
  the safety-relevant curvature is in the fiber dimension, not the
  capability magnitude.

- **Descent failure curvature.** The curvature between fiber-level balance
  and base-level oversight:

  ||F_∇(fiber_balance, base_oversight)|| = oversight gap for superhuman
    systems

  High curvature: fiber-level balance doesn't propagate to base-level
  oversight (the balance of power fails to protect humans because the
  projection loses relevant information). Low curvature: fiber balance
  implies base oversight (balance of power works). The article argues
  this curvature is high for superhuman systems.

- **Exit availability holonomy.** A loop through the ecosystem cycle
  (use service → depend on service → try to exit → barriers → stay)
  produces holonomy:

  Hol_γ(Γ_exit) = lock-in accumulation per usage cycle

  High holonomy: each cycle deepens lock-in (corporation-first
  architecture). Low or zero holonomy: exit remains easy (network-first
  architecture). The d-calculus holonomy is the formal measure of the
  "inversion test."
